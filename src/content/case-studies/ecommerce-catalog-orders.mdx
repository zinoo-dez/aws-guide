<section id="business-problem">
  <h2>1. Business Problem & Context</h2>
  <p>
    An e-commerce retailer tried to store both product catalog browsing (read-heavy, unstructured attributes) and checkout orders (write-heavy, strict ACID, payment ledgers) inside a single standard PostgreSQL database. During flash sales, browsing traffic consumed 100% of PostgreSQL connection pools, causing checkout payments to fail.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="requirements-constraints">
  <h2>2. Requirements & Constraints</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Catalog Throughput</strong>: Handle 50,000+ browsing queries/sec with sub-10ms latency.</li>
    <li><strong>Payment Integrity</strong>: Guarantee zero double-charges or corrupted inventory tallies.</li>
    <li><strong>Connection Protection</strong>: Prevent ephemeral serverless compute from overwhelming database connections.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section id="implementation">
  <h2>6. Implementation Highlights</h2>
  <DeepDiveAccordion title="Why RDS Proxy is Mandatory for Serverless RDBMS Access" badge="Architecture Guard">
    <p>
      Each AWS Lambda invocation container opens a dedicated database connection. If 1,000 Lambda functions spin up during a flash sale, opening 1,000 PostgreSQL connections will consume <strong>~10GB of DB RAM</strong> just for connection memory, crashing the database engine.
    </p>
    <p>
      <strong>AWS RDS Proxy</strong> sits between Lambda and Aurora, maintaining a pool of ~50 warm connections and multiplexing thousands of incoming Lambda queries across them seamlessly.
    </p>
  </DeepDiveAccordion>
</section>

<section id="takeaways">
  <h2>8. Key Architectural Takeaways</h2>
  <blockquote>
    <strong>Polyglot Rule:</strong> Use NoSQL (DynamoDB) where read velocity and flexible schemas dominate; use Relational (Aurora) where complex multi-table ACID transactions and financial guarantees are non-negotiable.
  </blockquote>
</section>
