<section id="business-problem">
  <h2>1. Business Problem & Context</h2>
  <p>
    Junior engineers and growing startups frequently encounter surprise four-figure bills due to left-running NAT Gateways, unattached Elastic IPs, provisioned IOPS disks, or recursive Lambda invocation loops.
  </p>
  <p>
    Standard AWS invoices are retroactive. Without proactive alerting, a runaway script over a holiday weekend can burn thousands of dollars before anyone notices.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="requirements-constraints">
  <h2>2. Requirements & Constraints</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Zero-Cost Alerting</strong>: Spend monitoring must not incur substantial infrastructure costs.</li>
    <li><strong>Multi-Threshold Triggers</strong>: Alerts at 50%, 80%, 100%, and forecasted 120% of monthly budget.</li>
    <li><strong>Automated Circuit Breaker</strong>: Option to automatically quarantine non-production resources if spend exceeds 150%.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section id="implementation">
  <h2>6. Implementation Highlights</h2>
  <DeepDiveAccordion title="Top 3 Free Tier Cost Traps to Watch" badge="FinOps Audit">
    <ul>
      <li><strong>NAT Gateways</strong>: Hourly charge (~$0.045/hr ≈ $32/mo per AZ) + data processing fee ($0.045/GB). Use VPC Endpoints for S3/DynamoDB instead.</li>
      <li><strong>Unattached Elastic IP Addresses</strong>: AWS charges for public IPv4 addresses that are provisioned but not associated with a running instance.</li>
      <li><strong>EBS Volumes without EC2 Instances</strong>: Terminating an EC2 instance may leave EBS disks orphaned if "Delete on Termination" is disabled.</li>
    </ul>
  </DeepDiveAccordion>
</section>
